## 1. What changed, in the app's terms

1. **Edit profile now saves.** `PUT /auth/profile` used to answer `200` and keep none of the name, phone, email or photo (it saved only the address and emergency-contact fields). Now **name and phone are saved**, the phone and the photo can be **cleared with an explicit `null`**, and what you send is what comes back. **The photo is still set only by uploading it** (`POST /auth/profile/upload-image`); `PUT` can only remove it.
2. **The email cannot be changed from the app yet.** It is the person's sign-in. Sending a different email is refused with a clear code. Show the email as read-only (both apps already do).
3. **Vitals are checked when saved.** Only the six known types, in their units, within plausible ranges. A reading the person mistyped (`300/20`) is now a `422` with a sentence to show.
4. **A patient can share their vitals with a caregiver, and take it back.** Grant, list and revoke now work (they always answered `403` or an empty list before). A caregiver can list the patients who shared with them.

## 3. Edit profile: `PUT /api/v1/auth/profile`

Send **only what changed**. The response is the flat profile (**no `patient`/`caregiver` object**): re-read `GET /auth/me` afterwards if the screen needs those.

### 3.0 What each value of each field means

| Field | Left out | `null` | `""` or blanks | A value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `full_name` | unchanged | `422` ("can't be removed") | `422` ("can't be blank") | trimmed, at most 120 characters |
| `phone_number` | unchanged | **clears it**, except on an account with **no email** (`400 phone_number_required`: the phone is its sign-in) | `422` (send `null` to clear) | 7 to 15 ASCII digits, optional leading `+`; spaces, `-`, `.` and brackets are **removed before saving** (`+234 000 071 8205` is saved as `+2340000718205`). Taken if **any other account holds the number in any spelling** (`+234803…`, `234803…`, `0803…`) |
| `profile_image_url` | unchanged | **clears it** | `422` | only the **value already stored** (sending it back is a no-op); any other URL is `400 photo_upload_only`. Upload new photos with `POST /auth/profile/upload-image` |
| `email` | unchanged | unchanged | `422` (not an email) | **the same email** (any letter case): unchanged. **A different email: `400 email_change_not_supported`** and nothing else in the request is saved |
| `password` | unchanged | unchanged | ignored | **`400 use_change_password`** (use `POST /auth/change-password`) |
| `address`, `gender`, `date_of_birth`, `emergency_contact_*` | unchanged | unchanged | **ignored (unchanged)** | saved on the patient or caregiver row, as before |

**A refused request saves nothing**, not even its valid fields.

The name and phone are saved on **three places at once**: the user, and the patient or caregiver row (which `/auth/me` shows under `patient` / `caregiver`), so the two never disagree. The phone is also what the person can sign in with, **exactly as saved**, so show the saved (separator-free) value back.

## 4. Vitals are checked when saved: `POST /api/v1/vitals/` and `PUT /api/v1/vitals/{id}`

The route and body are unchanged; **what is accepted is narrower**.

### 4.1 The rules

| `vital_type` | Unit (`unit`; the first is assumed if left out) | Value | Plausible range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `heart_rate` | `bpm` | a number | 20 to 250 |
| `blood_pressure` | `mmHg` | `systolic/diastolic`, e.g. `120/80` (spaces around `/` are tidied) | systolic 50 to 260, diastolic 30 to 160, **systolic higher than diastolic** |
| `temperature` | `°C` or `°F` | a number | 30 to 45 °C, 86 to 113 °F |
| `respiratory_rate` | `breaths/min` | a number | 4 to 60 |
| `blood_glucose` | `mg/dL` or `mmol/L` | a number | 20 to 600 mg/dL, 1.1 to 33.3 mmol/L |
| `sleep_duration` | `hrs` | a number of hours | more than 0, at most 24 |

- A **number** is digits with an optional decimal part: no sign, no comma (`36,8` is refused: send `36.8`), no exponent.
- **Glucose context** is not a separate type: it goes in `notes` as `RBS`, `FBS` or `PP`. **Sleep times** go in `notes` as `22:30-06:00`. `notes` is otherwise free text, at most 500 characters.
- The ranges are the patient app's own entry ranges (typing-mistake guards, **not clinical limits**: nothing here says a reading is normal or not). The caregiver app used to check only "a positive number", so it can now see a `422` it never saw before.
- **Only writes are checked.** Readings saved before this (a `banana`, a `300/20`) are still listed, downloaded and deleted as before; the apps already skip types they do not know.

## 5. Who can see a patient's vitals, and sharing them

### 5.1 The rules (unchanged, and now pinned by tests)

A caregiver can read and write a patient's vitals **only** while a visit with that patient is **`in_progress`**, **or** while the patient has an active grant to that caregiver. Accepted, on-the-way, completed and cancelled visits give nothing. The patient can always read their own. Everyone else gets `403` `{"detail": "Access denied"}`.

**A revoke does not end an in-progress visit's access.** The two reasons are independent: revoking removes the grant, the visit still gives access until it ends (`330`).

### 5.2 Grant: `POST /api/v1/vitals/access/grant` (patient only)

Body: `patient_id` = the patient's **own** `patients.id`, `caregiver_id` = the caregiver's `caregivers.id` (the caregiver's id is on the booking: `caregiver_id`). Granting twice returns the same grant. The caregiver can read straight away.

<!-- capture: 301-grant.json request.body -->
```json
{
  "patient_id": "788d7059-801c-43b5-8a39-ff9b2bca14f8",
  "caregiver_id": "39bd28b4-cf03-4e68-8188-0d22ca5564b4"
}
```

`caregiver_name` is the name to show in the patient's list. `patient_name` is also present (the patient's own name).

Before the grant the caregiver was refused (`300`, `403 Access denied`); after it they read the vitals (`305`); **another** caregiver is still refused (`306`).

### 5.3 List: `GET /api/v1/vitals/access/list`

Active grants only (revoked ones are not listed). **A patient** gets the caregivers they shared with; **a caregiver** gets the patients who shared with them. Same shape; an empty list is `[]`.

<!-- capture: 303-patient-lists-grants.json response.body -->
```json

  {
    "patient_id": "788d7059-801c-43b5-8a39-ff9b2bca14f8",
```

## 7. Field-nesting and shape traps

- **The profile PUT response is flat**; `/auth/me` nests `patient` / `caregiver`. The name and phone are on both after a save.
- **`email` is read-only.** Do not send it. If you do send the current one it is ignored; any other value fails the **whole** request.
- **`null` clears, `""` is an error** for the phone and photo (a photo is only ever *set* by upload), and a name can never be cleared. `""` for `address`, `gender` and the emergency fields is **ignored**, not an error (unchanged behaviour): clearing those is still not possible.
- **The phone is stored without separators.** Compare what you sent with what came back, not with what the person typed.
- **Ids:** see section 2. The access endpoints take profile ids, the vitals `recorded_by_user_id` takes the user id.
- **The vitals refusal list is a `422` list, the profile and access refusals are objects or strings.** Do not assume one shape.

## 8. UX consequences the app must surface

1. **Edit profile:** show the server's `detail[0].msg` (422) or `detail.message` (400/409) under the field or in the banner. Do not show "Saved" without a `200`.
2. **Email stays read-only** with a line such as "To change your email, contact support". If a `400 email_change_not_supported` ever arrives, show its `message` and keep the old email.
3. **Changing the phone is unverified for now** (no code is sent to the new number), so never present it as proof of ownership. **Clearing a phone number** is a deliberate action ("Remove phone number" sends `null`). An emptied text box should be treated as "no change" or a validation error on the screen, never sent as `""`.
4. **The patient app's "not saved" workaround** (comparing what was sent with `/auth/me`) is no longer needed for name and phone; keep the re-read of `/auth/me` for the `patient` object.
5. **Vitals entry:** keep the apps' own range checks (they are the same ranges); on a `422` show `detail[0].msg` and keep the person's input on screen. The caregiver app will now see `422` for readings outside the ranges (a heart rate of 300, a temperature of 50 °C) that it used to save.
6. **Sharing vitals** is health-data consent: confirm with the patient before granting ("[name] will be able to see and add to your vitals until you remove them"); show each active grant by `caregiver_name` with a "Stop sharing" action; after revoking, tell the patient it took effect immediately.
7. **A revoke does not hide the vitals from a caregiver who is in the middle of a visit with that patient.** If the screen implies otherwise, say so: "They can still see your vitals while their visit is in progress."
8. **The caregiver's "patients who shared their vitals" list** shows `patient_name`; opening one reads `/vitals/{patient_id}` and can answer `403` if the patient revoked meanwhile: handle it as "No longer shared".
