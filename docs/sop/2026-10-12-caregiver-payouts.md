## What shipped
- **Self-withdrawal is gone.** `POST /caregiver/wallet/withdraw` now answers `410` ("Withdrawals are no longer available. Your available balance is paid to your bank account automatically on the payout schedule.") and moves nothing. It stays as a 410 (not a 404) so an installed app that still shows the button explains itself.
- **Bank details are verified.** `GET /caregiver/banks` (Paystack `GET /bank?country=nigeria`, cached in the process for a day), `POST /caregiver/bank-account/resolve` (Paystack `/bank/resolve`, 10 lookups a minute per caregiver), and `PUT /caregiver/bank-account` which takes only `bank_code` and `account_number`, resolves the holder's name itself, registers a Paystack transfer recipient (`/transferrecipient`) and stores the name and `recipient_code`. Changing to a **different** account, or saving the **first** account while the wallet already holds money, blocks payouts for 24 hours and writes a `caregiver_bank_events` row (with `payout_blocked_until`).
- **Scheduled payouts.** A daily ARQ job (`payout_batch_job`, 06:00:10 UTC) makes one batch on a payout day (`payout_cadence`: weekly = Mondays, biweekly = Mondays of even ISO weeks, monthly = the 1st). For each caregiver whose available balance is at least `payout_min_naira` (5,000 by default) and who has a verified recipient, is not suspended or erased, is not cooling down and has no live payout, it **takes the whole available balance out of the wallet** under the wallet lock and writes it as a `payout` debit (`pending_approval`) plus a `payouts` row.
- **Admin routes** under `/admin/payouts`: list batches and payouts (`payments.read`); approve a batch, cancel a batch or a payout (before approval), retry a failed or reversed payout, resolve a stuck payout (`ledger.adjust`, reason required, audited). Approving re-checks every caregiver and cancels (returning the money) those no longer eligible.
- **Sending and settling.** `payout_dispatch_job` (every minute) posts one `POST /transfer` per approved payout with the payout's own reference. `transfer.success`, `transfer.failed` and `transfer.reversed` webhooks (existing signature check, idempotent) and a reconcile job (`payout_reconcile_job`, every 15 minutes, asks `GET /transfer/verify/<reference>`) settle it. Failed and reversed payouts return the money to the available balance.
- **Hardening after the money review** (the same commits; items numbered as in the review).
  1. **A success that arrives after we returned the money no longer pays twice.** New payout status **`needs_attention`**. On `transfer.success` for a payout already `failed`, `reversed` or `cancelled`, the returned amount is **debited from the balance again** (as much as is there; the rest goes to `payouts.shortfall_kobo`), the ledger row becomes `successful`, an error is logged and the payout is `needs_attention`. What else that does (freezing the caregiver) is in "The invariant"; `retry_payout` refuses a `needs_attention` payout.
  2. **Attempts are rows.** New table `payout_attempts` (`payout_id`, `attempt`, unique `reference`, `status`, `failure_reason`, `created_at`). Every send creates one; webhook and reconcile events are matched by **attempt reference**, then the payout. Success on a non-current attempt is the item 1 handling (`needs_attention`; the money is taken back if it was returned, and stays reserved if a retry holds it, in which case the retry is never sent). A retry no longer overwrites history: the failed attempt keeps its `failure_reason`. **Retry first calls `verify_transfer` on the current attempt** and proceeds only on a definite failed or reversed answer; pending, OTP, no record and unreachable are refused with a `409` and a plain message; a `success` answer settles the payout as `needs_attention` and refuses the retry.
  3. **Reconcile no longer returns money on silence.** "No record at Paystack 30 minutes after the send" leaves the money reserved and sets `needs_attention` (and freezes the caregiver) with the reason "Paystack has no record; check before releasing".
  4. **The send job re-checks the caregiver** when it claims a payout: `profile_status` approved or active, `user.is_active`, not erased. Otherwise the payout is cancelled as the system (`cancelled_by` null, reason "Cancelled before sending: ..."), the money returned, nothing sent.
  5. **`POST /admin/payouts/{id}/resolve`** (`{outcome: paid|failed, reason, force}`, `ledger.adjust`, audited as `payout.resolve`) for `processing` (for example stuck at OTP) and `needs_attention` payouts, and **`POST /admin/payouts/batches/{id}/cancel`** (audited as `payout_batch.cancel`) which returns every `pending_approval` payout's money. Resolve asks Paystack first and refuses an outcome a definite answer contradicts, `force` or not; an answer that is not final needs `force: true`.
  6. **First bank account with money in the wallet** starts the same 24 hour cool-down as a change.
  7. The worker comment now names the real batch time (06:00:10 UTC).
- **Second money review (same PR).** Two of its items remain as shipped: **`initiate_transfer` treats `408` and `409` as unclear** (like a 5xx or 429): the payout stays `processing` for the webhook or reconcile; other 4xx still refuse and return the money (scoped to the transfer call in `app/services/paystack.py`). Its other three items (a late success while a newer payout is live, a reversal after a shortfall, a success with a different amount) were handled case by case and are **superseded by the invariant below**.
- **Third money review: the invariant.** See the next section.
- `GET /caregiver/wallet` adds `payout_cadence`, `payout_min_kobo`, `next_payout_on`, `payout_in_progress_kobo`, `payouts_blocked_until`; `total_withdrawn` now counts successful payouts; transactions gain `type: payout` with statuses `pending_approval`, `pending`, `successful`, `failed`, `cancelled`. Account erasure is refused while a payout is in flight and masks payout destinations.

## The invariant

**Once a caregiver's payouts are disputed, automatic code may only DECREASE what the caregiver can be paid, never increase it. Money returns only through an explicit, audited admin action.** The third review found a new double payment in each round (a take-back, then netting, then a failure that returned money which had already covered another payout's shortfall), all from automatic money moves between payouts after a late Paystack success. The per-case handling was replaced by one rule (`app/services/payouts.py`, module docstring).

- **The freeze.** `caregiver_wallets.payouts_frozen_at` and `payouts_frozen_reason` (migration `0012`, edited in place: unreleased, no data). It is on the wallet because every money move already takes the wallet lock, so the freeze is read and written under the same lock (`payout_blocked_until` lives on the bank account because it is about the account, not the money). A freeze keeps its first reason.
- **What is a contradiction** (Paystack's word disagrees with what we recorded): a success after `failed` / `reversed` / `cancelled`; a success on a non-current attempt, on a payout that was never sent, or a second success; a success for a different amount (the payout is flagged `needs_attention` **whatever its status**, money untouched, so the unfreeze guard sees it); the reconcile job finding Paystack has no record; a reversal (or failure) after a success, **including a failure or reversal after an admin forced a payout to `paid`** (`payout_attempts.forced`: Paystack never said it was paid, so its failure is a contradiction, not a stale duplicate; nothing is credited). Each **freezes the caregiver and sets the payouts involved to `needs_attention`**.
- **What the code may then do with money, and nothing else:** (1) cancel a newer payout that was not sent and let its reservation pay the take-back (money-neutral: it lowers what will be paid); (2) debit whatever `wallet.balance` holds, up to the amount owed; (3) record the outstanding shortfall in `payouts.shortfall_kobo`. **It never credits the wallet.** Every shortfall and return amount is clamped to `>= 0`; the `CHECK (balance >= 0)` stays.
- **While frozen:** the batch excludes the caregiver (candidate query, and again under the wallet lock in `_reserve`); the send job skips their payouts; approval cancels their unsent payouts; retry is refused. Every webhook or reconcile event on ANY of their payouts records the attempt outcome and the payout's status but performs **no wallet credit**: a failed or reversed payout becomes `needs_attention` and keeps its reservation; a success just settles it (the reservation becomes the payment, the balance does not move). A reversal after success sets the ledger row back to `pending` (the money is in limbo, not credited).
- **`needs_attention` is no longer in `uq_payouts_one_active_per_caregiver`** (blocking batches is the freeze's job); the index covers `pending_approval` and `processing`. `needs_attention` stays a status.
- **Admin actions (all `ledger.adjust`, audited).** `POST /admin/payouts/{id}/resolve` as `failed` **nets** the reservation against the caregiver's outstanding shortfall while they are frozen: returned = `max(0, reserved - outstanding shortfall)`, the shortfall goes down by what is kept (oldest payout first); `returned_kobo` and `netted_kobo` are on the payout and in the ledger row's `status_reason` (and the audit entry). A `refused` attempt with Paystack's "no record" now counts as a definite failure for resolve, as it already did for retry. `POST /admin/payouts/caregivers/{caregiver_id}/unfreeze` `{reason, write_off_shortfall}` is refused while any of the caregiver's payouts is `needs_attention` or `processing`. **Earnings keep accruing while frozen, so the unfreeze first DEBITS the outstanding shortfall from `wallet.balance` (up to what is there; it only lowers the balance) and lowers the shortfall by that amount** (oldest payout first; each shortfall payout's ledger row gets a `status_reason` note, the audit row `debited_from_balance_kobo` and `per_payout`). A shortfall that is still left refuses the unfreeze with `409` (nothing changes, the debit is rolled back too) unless `write_off_shortfall` is true, which writes it off and records the amount in the audit row. After a successful unfreeze no shortfall is left on record. `resolve` is money-safe for a payout flagged after its money was returned: as `failed` it just closes the payout (nothing moves), as `paid` it takes the returned money back from the balance and records the rest as shortfall.
- **Check-then-act races (fourth review).** `resolve_payout` and `retry_payout` ask Paystack with no lock held, so what the answer depends on is read again under the locks. `resolve`: after locking the payout it locks ALL its attempts (by attempt number, the settle order: payout, attempts, ledger row, wallet) and re-checks them: resolving as `failed` is refused with `409` if any attempt is now `success`; resolving as `paid` is refused if an attempt other than the verified one is `success`, or the verified attempt's state changed; the shortfall and netting are computed from the rows locked at that point. Before this, a late success for attempt 1 committed during the Paystack call for attempt 2 made `resolve failed` credit the whole reservation (an overpayment), and the mirror made `resolve paid` pay twice. `retry`: the wallet is locked first and eligibility, the freeze included, is read after it (a contradiction freezes under the wallet lock), so a freeze committed after the old checks could not be missed.
- **Races.** The newer payout is **re-queried after the wallet lock** (`_locate`): a batch takes the wallet lock first, so one it created between a late success locking its own payout and getting the wallet is found and cancelled (test: `test_a_payout_reserved_while_a_late_success_waits_for_the_wallet_is_still_cancelled`). Every event locks the caregiver's live payouts in id order, then their ledger rows, then the wallet. Erasure (`account_erasure.py`) now takes the payout and ledger rows before the wallet, the same order, and is refused while a caregiver is frozen.
- **Alternatives rejected.** Keeping the per-case handling and adding cases (three rounds each found another); making `needs_attention` payouts block batches through the unique index (it also blocked the send path for the newer payout and forced "close P1 as success, flag P2" hacks); crediting a failure while frozen and relying on a later take-back (a success can arrive after the money is spent); putting the freeze on the bank account (the lock that matters is the wallet's).
- **Wallet response.** Left unchanged: exposing the freeze to the caregiver app would mean re-capturing the caregiver guide and deciding the wording. The admin payout objects carry `caregiver_payouts_frozen_at` / `caregiver_payouts_frozen_reason`.

## Why
Self-withdrawal moved money out with no human in the loop and no integration with the bank; the owner wants a weekly, reviewed payout run. Money leaving Oakly must be approved by a person, reserved so it cannot be spent twice, and settled from Paystack's word, never guessed.

## How
| Decision | Choice | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Reservation | The payout amount is **debited from `caregiver_wallets.balance` when the payout is made**, as a `payout` ledger row in `pending_approval`. The balance and the ledger always agree; a second batch, a retry or a bank change can never spend the same money. A `CHECK (balance >= 0)` on the wallet is a last line of defence. | Reading the balance at approval time (stale, and a race) |
| One live payout per caregiver | Partial unique index on `payouts(caregiver_id)` where status is `pending_approval` or `processing` (`needs_attention` is not in it: the freeze blocks batches). | Application check only |
| One batch per payout date | `payout_batches.period_key` (the ISO date) is unique; the job can run twice. Batch creation is **one transaction** (batch and payouts appear whole), wallets locked in sorted caregiver order, each reservation inside a savepoint. | Commit per caregiver (a batch could be approved half built) |
| Lock order | batch, then payout, then ledger row, then wallets in sorted order: the existing booking, transaction, wallet order. Paystack is never called while a lock is held. | |
| Sending | Approving only flips status. The send job **claims** a payout (`initiated_at`, committed) before the HTTP call, so two workers cannot send one; the reference `po-<payout id>-<attempt>` is unique per attempt (Paystack refuses a reused one). | Calling Paystack inside the approve request (slow, locks held) |
| Outcomes | A definite 4xx refusal fails the payout (attempt `refused`) and returns the money. **No answer, a 5xx, a 408, a 409 or a 429 from `POST /transfer` leaves it `processing` with the money reserved**: the transfer may exist. The webhook or the reconcile job decides. Reconcile fails a transfer only if Paystack says failed/reversed; **no record 30 minutes after we sent it makes it `needs_attention` (caregiver frozen) with the money still reserved**, because the transfer may yet exist. | Treating a timeout as a failure (could pay twice after a retry) |
| OTP | Paystack's `otp` status leaves the payout `processing` with a note in `failure_reason`. **Paystack OTP must be disabled for API transfers before go-live.** An admin settles it with `POST /admin/payouts/{id}/resolve`. | Failing it (the transfer could be finalised later, paying twice) |
| Settlement | `apply_transfer_event` transitions from one state only; a repeat is a no-op. The ordinary transitions are `processing -> success/failed/reversed`; anything that contradicts what was recorded freezes the caregiver instead (see "The invariant"). Every event is matched by attempt reference. | Last-write-wins |
| Re-check at approval and at send | The send job repeats the caregiver check (approved or active, account open, not erased) because approval can be hours earlier. |
| Re-check at approval | Suspended, erased, or bank account changed since the batch was made (or a cool-down running) means the payout is cancelled and the money returned, not sent. Retry applies the same checks plus "the balance is still there" and "Paystack confirms the current attempt failed". | Paying the account snapshot |
| Bank change | Server resolves the holder name (a client-sent name is ignored); the same account again is not a "change" and an empty wallet's first account has no cool-down, but a first account saved while the wallet holds money starts one (whoever holds the login could otherwise name an account and collect the balance). An account saved before this release has no recipient, so it is **not paid until saved again**. Bank save takes the wallet lock first, then the bank row, the same order batch creation reads them in. | Trusting the client's `account_name` |
| Audit | The admin audit log needs an admin, so the caregiver's own bank change goes to a new `caregiver_bank_events` table (masked numbers, ip, user agent). Admin approve, cancel and retry write `admin_audit_log` rows. Webhook and job settlements have no admin and write none (the payout row is their record). | Making `admin_audit_log.admin_user_id` nullable |
| Permissions | List: `payments.read`. Approve, cancel (payout or batch), retry, resolve: `ledger.adjust` (**Super Admin only today**, as the owner specified). Finance cannot approve. `payouts.execute` (Finance) exists in the catalogue but is not used. | |
| Bank-list cache and lookup limiter | Per process (in-memory), not Redis: the app has no shared limiter, and the cost of a duplicate fetch is trivial. | Redis cache |
| Policy | `payout_cadence` and `payout_min_naira` are read **live** (no per-booking snapshot), as agreed. A zero minimum still never makes an empty payout. | |

## Operate / roll back
- The worker must run (`docker compose up -d` includes it). Jobs: `payout_batch_job` (daily 06:00:10 UTC, acts only on a payout day), `payout_dispatch_job` (every minute), `payout_reconcile_job` (minutes 7, 22, 37, 52). By hand: `from app.services import payouts; payouts.create_batch(SessionLocal(), period_key="2026-10-19")`, `payouts.dispatch_due(SessionLocal())`, `payouts.reconcile(SessionLocal())`. If the daily job missed a payout day nothing is created until the next one.
- **Before the first real payout:** (1) run one payout against a live Paystack **test** account end to end (transfer, webhook, balance, bank list, name lookup); (2) disable OTP for API transfers in the Paystack dashboard; (3) top up the Paystack balance (a transfer beyond it is refused, the payout fails, an admin retries); (4) confirm the webhook URL receives `transfer.*` events.
- Changing `payout_cadence` or `payout_min_naira` (`PATCH /admin/booking-policy`) takes effect at the next job run.
- Roll back: `alembic downgrade -1` drops the new tables and deletes `payout` ledger rows. A payout that was reserved but not paid would then look available again, and money Paystack already paid is not undone. **Once real payouts exist, do not downgrade; reconcile by hand or write a forward migration.** (Greenfield now, so acceptable today.) To stop payouts without a rollback: do not approve the batch, or cancel its payouts; or set `payout_min_naira` very high.

## Follow-ups
- A live Paystack test (not done); OTP disabled for transfers (not done).
- No route to make a batch by hand after a missed payout day.
- **Erasure with a balance between 0 and 5,000.00 NGN, and reversals after erasure.** Erasure is refused while a payout is pending, but a caregiver whose balance is under the minimum can be erased with the money in the wallet, and a `reversed` event or a late success for an erased caregiver finds no bank account to pay. These need a one-off payout or manual handling (a ledger adjustment).
- **Batch creation holds every eligible wallet lock until a single commit.** Fine at today's volume; as the caregiver count grows consider per-caregiver commits (a batch is then built in pieces and must be approved only when complete) or smaller chunks.
- **Alert on old `pending_approval` batches** (the money of every caregiver in it is reserved and unavailable until someone approves or cancels), and on any `needs_attention` payout.
- A `shortfall_kobo` is recovered only at unfreeze, from the earnings that built up while frozen (debited, then written off if the balance cannot cover it); there is no automatic debit of earnings while a caregiver is frozen or after the unfreeze.
- **Earnings keep accruing while a caregiver is frozen. This is intended** (the money is theirs; only payouts are held, and the unfreeze nets the shortfall against them). Decide whether the caregiver app should say so.
- **The narrow `_locate` re-query deadlock.** `_locate` locks the payout, attempts and ledger rows, then the wallet, then re-queries the caregiver's live payouts for one a batch created in between. The batch takes the wallet lock first, then inserts and locks its payout, so a batch holding the wallet and waiting for a row an event already holds, while that event waits for the wallet, deadlocks. Postgres aborts one side and Paystack retries the webhook (or the batch job reruns), so no money moves wrongly, but it is noisy; fix by taking the wallet lock before the row locks in `_locate` (it needs the caregiver id first, which one extra read gives) once the lock-order test exists.
- `resolve` as `paid` for a payout whose money had been returned takes it back from the balance only; it does not also cancel a newer unsent payout's reservation as the take-back after a late success does (the leftover is recorded as shortfall and nets against that payout's resolve).
- A caregiver balance below the minimum can never be paid or delete their account: needs an admin sweep or a ledger adjustment (AD-FR-42).
- Finance cannot approve payouts (decide whether Finance gets `payouts.execute`).
- The lookup limiter and bank-list cache are per process (Redis if the API scales out); compare the resolved name with the caregiver's profile name.
- Console screens for batches and payouts, and the caregiver app's payout and bank-account screens (the app is dropping the withdraw button in parallel).
- Slice 3: refunds v2 and ledger adjustments; an earning or payout for a booking refunded after completion does not claw back.
- **Webhook has no recipient check.** `apply_transfer_event` matches on our attempt reference and amount but does not compare the event's recipient with the payout's `recipient_code`. The HMAC signature check makes this low risk; add the comparison if Paystack ever sends events from other integrations.
- **`resolve(failed, force)` does not re-check `initiated_at`.** An admin forcing `failed` on a payout that was sent but unanswered releases money that may still be paid. Relies on the admin's reason and the Paystack check; consider refusing it when `initiated_at` is set and Paystack's answer is not final.
- **The first-save cool-down ignores earnings still clearing.** It looks at the wallet balance only, so a first bank account saved while earnings are still in the clearing period does not start a cool-down.
- A late success while the newer payout was already sent is an overpayment Oakly cannot prevent: it is recorded as `shortfall_kobo`, both payouts are `needs_attention`, and the admin nets or writes it off. Recovering it from future earnings is manual.
- The wallet response does not show the freeze; a frozen caregiver's unsent `processing` payout is never sent and is settled with `resolve` (it needs `force`, since Paystack has no record); consider a cancel route for it. Alert on any frozen caregiver.
