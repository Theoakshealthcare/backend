## 1. What the caregiver app does, in the app's terms

1. **Sign up and get approved.** The caregiver registers, verifies their email, fills in two profile steps, uploads a licence and accepts the consents. That puts them in `under_review`. An admin decides. Only `approved` caregivers can go available or accept visits.
2. **Go available.** A switch on the home screen. While on, and while the app keeps reporting a fresh location, the caregiver is offered new visits.
3. **Receive offers.** When a patient pays, the backend offers the booking to up to 8 nearby caregivers (a "wave"), then 8 more every 10 minutes, for at most 30 minutes. Each notified caregiver gets a push ("New visit request"). The first to accept wins; everyone else gets `409`.
4. **Run the visit.** Accept, then **On my way**, then enter the **4-digit visit code** the patient reads out at the door (**Start**), then **Complete**.
5. **Get paid.** A completed visit credits a pending earning that clears after a hold (24 hours by default) and then shows in the available balance. **There is no withdrawal:** on each payout day (weekly by default) Oakly reserves the available balance, an admin approves, and it is paid to the caregiver's verified bank account. See section 10.

## 2. Conventions

| Topic | What is true | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Error shape | FastAPI's `{"detail": ...}`. It is **not** `{"success": false, "error": ...}`. `detail` is a **string** for almost every error, a **list of objects** for `422` validation errors, one **object** for the admin approve `409` (not a caregiver-app call), and an **object `{"code", "message"}`** for every identity-verification error (section 4). | Sections 3 to 10 |
| Auth | `Authorization: Bearer <access_token>`. A refresh token sent as a bearer is rejected with `401` `Could not validate credentials`. | 010 |
| No token | `401` `{"detail": "Not authenticated"}` | 009 |
| Wrong account type | `403` `{"detail": "Access denied. Caregiver account required."}` (a patient token on a caregiver endpoint) | 044, 082, 132 |
| Timestamps | ISO 8601 **UTC without a `Z` or offset**, for example `2026-10-06T00:03:09.513867`. Treat them as UTC. The only exception is the push payload's `scheduled_at`, which ends in `Z`. | many |
| IDs | UUID strings. | many |
| Token lifetimes | Access token 60 minutes, refresh token 7 days. **Not measured live**: these are the configured defaults (`ACCESS_TOKEN_EXPIRE_MINUTES`, `REFRESH_TOKEN_EXPIRE_DAYS`). | config |
| Money | Mixed units; see the traps in section 12. | |

### Delete account: `DELETE /api/v1/auth/profile`
Auth required (any account type; the caregiver app uses the caregiver's token). No request body. This is an **erasure request handled as anonymisation**: the person's data is wiped and the account is closed for good, but bookings, wallet history, consents and support tickets are kept for legal reasons. There is no undo.

**After a successful delete, with the old tokens and the old email** (captures `140` to `144`, caregiver E):

| Call | Status | Body |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /auth/me` with the old access token | `403` | `{"detail": "Inactive user"}` |
| `POST /auth/refresh` with the old refresh token | `400` | `{"detail": "Inactive user"}` |
| `POST /auth/login/mobile` with the old email | `401` | `{"detail": "Incorrect email/phone or password"}` |
| `POST /auth/register` with the same email again | `200` | a brand-new account (new `id`, `is_verified: false`); nothing of the old one carries over |

What is erased and what stays (from the code and tests; the live capture shows only the account row, `db-erased-account-row`): name becomes `Deleted user`, email becomes a placeholder, phone, address, date of birth, emergency contact, profile image, bank account, and the ID and face verification data are removed. Bookings, wallet transactions (payout account numbers are masked to the last four digits), consents and support tickets stay.

### `profile_status` state machine

| State | Meaning | What the app shows |
|---|---|---|
| `incomplete` | Onboarding not finished. | Onboarding screens. |
| `under_review` | Submitted; waiting for an admin. | "Under review". |
| `changes_requested` | Admin asked for fixes. `changes_requested.items` and `.note` say what. | The list of items to fix and a resubmit action (resubmitting consent returns the caregiver to `under_review`). |
| `rejected` | Admin rejected. `rejection_reason` says why. | The reason. Final. |
| `approved` | Can go available and accept visits. | The app. |
| `suspended` | Admin suspended. | A suspended notice; cannot go available or accept. |

### Identity verification (KYC): `/caregiver/identity-verification/...`

**What it is.** After Step 2 the caregiver proves who they are with a hosted check (BVN or NIN plus a live selfie) run by our verification partner, **Dojah** (the partner was Prembly until 2026-10-30; nothing in the app's contract mentions a partner name). The check runs inside Dojah's widget; the photos go from the phone to Dojah and never reach Oakly. **The app never decides the outcome.** The result reaches the backend from Dojah directly (a signed webhook, plus a periodic check by the backend itself); the app only reads the status. Everything the widget reports back to the app (opened, completed, closed, error) is sent to the backend as a *hint* and changes nothing about the caregiver's identity status. **The backend creates the widget's `reference_id` when it creates the attempt, before the widget opens; the app passes it to the widget and never makes one up.**

**Captured by the backend test harness (the real app and a real Postgres), not the compose stack: captures `200` to `231` come from `app/tests/test_capture_kyc_caregiver.py`. NOT live against Dojah, and this matters:** the decision engine (backend slices K4 and KD1) is real code, and the captures marked **[engine]** were produced by running it (the same functions the worker and the cron run) over verifications served by a **fake Dojah** whose shapes come from Dojah's public docs and from the Timp and Weysure integrations. Nobody at Oakly has yet seen a real Dojah verification, webhook or widget callback (the K8 live pass is still ahead), so *what real results look like, how many real caregivers land in review, the exact face scores, and whether the widget accepts our `reference_id` as given* are unconfirmed. Captures marked **[seeded]** put an attempt row straight into the database. The "is Dojah up" flag was an in-memory stand-in for the Redis flag (the engine wrote it through the same code path). The app id, public key and widget id in the examples are placeholders, and so is the `reference_id` (its `OKL-` + 32 hex shape is real, the value is not). The widget itself (app slices K7a/K7b) is not built.

All five calls need a caregiver token. A patient token gets `403 Access denied. Caregiver account required.` and no token gets `401 Not authenticated` (captures `224`, `225`).

|---|---|

**Open the check through `widget_url` (the app does not embed Dojah's SDK).** `widget_url` is an absolute https link to a page the backend hosts (`<public base>/api/v1/kyc/widget/<token>`). ## 7. Offers and bookings

### Booking `status` and `dispatch_state`

`status` (the client enum gained one value, `en_route`):

| `status` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Paid, not yet accepted (or unpaid). |
| `accepted` | A caregiver accepted. |
| `en_route` | **New.** The caregiver pressed On my way. |
| `in_progress` | Started (visit code entered). |
| `completed` | Done. |
| `cancelled` | Cancelled (by the caregiver, by expiry or payment failure). |
| `declined` | Exists in the enum; the caregiver `decline` action does **not** set it (see below). |

`dispatch_state` (separate from `status`; now also in `BookingResponse`):

| `dispatch_state` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `null` | Not dispatched: unpaid, or paid before dispatch existed. |
| `dispatching` | Paid and being offered in waves. |
| `matched` | A caregiver accepted. |
| `unmatched` | 30 minutes passed with no taker. Offers already sent can still be accepted. |

Transitions caregivers cause: `pending` -> `accepted` (accept) -> `en_route` (on_my_way, optional) -> `in_progress` (start, needs the code) -> `completed` (complete). `cancel` is allowed from `accepted`, `en_route` and `in_progress`. `decline` changes nothing on the booking: it only removes that caregiver's offer. Anything else is `409`.

### List and detail

| Need | Call |
|---|---|
| Offers for the home screen | `open_offers` in `GET /caregiver/home` (minimal, no patient data) |
| My visits | `GET /booking/caregiver/bookings?status=accepted` (also `en_route`, `in_progress`, `completed`, `cancelled`, `pending`) |
| Raw offers | `GET /booking/caregiver/bookings?status=new_requests` and `GET /booking/caregiver/notifications` |
| One booking | `GET /booking/booking/{id}` (only the assigned caregiver, the booking's patient or an admin token with `bookings.read`) |

**An offer is not yet the caregiver's job, so it carries no patient information.** In `new_requests`, `/caregiver/notifications` and the `decline` response, a booking the caregiver is **not assigned to** comes back as an *offer view*: `patient_details` is `null`, `condition` and `special_instructions` are `null`, `patient_address` is only the area (`"<city>, <state>"`, e.g. `"Lagos, Lagos"`), and `patient_latitude`/`patient_longitude` are rounded to 2 decimals (about 1 km), enough for a distance. Once the caregiver accepts and is the assigned caregiver, the same booking returns the full details (name, phone, exact address, condition). Build the offers screen from these fields only; never expect patient identity before acceptance.

### Cancelling: the rule depends on the booking's `channel`

Every caregiver booking response now carries `channel`: `"app"`, `"whatsapp"` or `"admin"` (a string, never null, at the top level of the booking; the offer view shows it too). Read it to decide which cancel behaviour to show. The field is on the typed booking (`GET /booking/booking/{id}`, `GET /booking/caregiver/bookings`) and inside the action response's `booking`.

| `channel` | `cancel` from the app |
|---|---|
| `app` or `admin` | Allowed from `accepted`, `en_route` and `in_progress`. The booking becomes `cancelled`, `cancel_reason: "caregiver_cancelled"` (the `reason` text is discarded). Unchanged. |
| `whatsapp` | Allowed **only** while the booking is `accepted` **and** more than the policy's `dropout_redispatch_min_lead_minutes` (120 by default) before `scheduled_date`. Then the booking is **reopened for a replacement**: the response's `booking.status` is `pending`, `caregiver_id` is `null`, and it leaves the caregiver's list. Otherwise `409`, below. |

## 10. Wallet, bank account and payouts

All amounts on these endpoints are **integer kobo** (100 kobo = 1 naira). `GET /caregiver/home` is the only caregiver endpoint that gives naira.

**Caregivers no longer withdraw.** `POST /caregiver/wallet/withdraw` is gone (it answers `410`, below). Oakly pays each caregiver's **available balance** to their bank account on a schedule, after an admin approves the batch. The app should have no withdraw button; it shows the balance, the bank account and where the next payout stands.

### 10.1 How money moves

1. **Earn.** Completing a visit credits the caregiver **one earning**: 80% of the booking's **service fee** (the admin can change the percent; it is copied onto each booking when it is created). It starts `pending_clearance` and does **not** touch `balance`. 24 hours after completion (also admin-editable) a background job moves it to the available balance. An admin can put a pending earning on `held` (for example after a no-show flag or a dispute) or `voided`. A late family cancellation that the caregiver was set out for in good time pays a share of the fee kept the same way (`type: "late_cancel_compensation"`).
2. **Payout day.** On a payout day (weekly = every Monday by default; the admin can switch to `biweekly` or `monthly`; the wallet tells you which) a background job makes a batch at 06:00 UTC. A caregiver gets a payout when their available balance is **at least `payout_min_kobo`** (500000 = 5,000.00 NGN by default, admin-editable), they have a **verified bank account**, they are not suspended, the account was **not changed in the last 24 hours**, and they have no payout in progress. The payout is the **whole available balance**.
3. **Reserved.** The moment the payout is made its amount **leaves `available_balance`** and shows as `payout_in_progress_kobo` and as a `payout` row (`pending_approval`) in the transaction list. Nothing has been sent yet.
4. **Approved and sent.** An admin approves the batch (it can take a working day; there is no fixed deadline). The row becomes `pending` and the send job pays it through Paystack. The money normally reaches the bank within minutes, but that depends on Paystack and the bank.
5. **Settled.** Paystack's word settles it: the row becomes `successful` (counted in `total_withdrawn`), or `failed` (the money is back in `available_balance` and an admin can retry it), or `cancelled` (an admin cancelled it before approval, or the caregiver was no longer eligible at approval; the money is back in `available_balance`).

**[seeded]** An AVAILABLE balance of 25,000.00 NGN was inserted into the database so the flow could be shown (capture `121-db-seeded-wallet-balance.json`); the pending earning in the captures is real, created by the visit completed earlier in the run. **[db-simulated]** The 24-hour wait was replaced by moving `available_at` into the past; the release job itself is the real function, run once in the worker container. **[simulated schedule]** The batch step is the real function (`create_batch`) run once in the worker container for today's date, standing in for the daily cron that acts only on a payout day (`worker-payout-batch-job`, capture 167). **[fake Paystack]** Paystack was a local fake in every capture of this section: the bank list, the account-name lookup, the recipient, the transfer and the transfer webhooks are the fake's answers, shaped after Paystack's documented replies. **A live Paystack account has not been exercised.**

**UX consequences the app must surface.**
- **No withdraw button.** Replace it with the schedule: "Your available balance is paid out every Monday" (from `payout_cadence`; weekly = Monday), "Next payout: <next_payout_on>", and "Minimum payout: <payout_min_kobo formatted>". - A payout is **not instant and not automatic**: it needs an admin's approval. Show `pending_approval` as "Payout being prepared" and `pending` as "Payout on its way to your bank". Only `successful` is "Paid". Never call it paid before.
- The reserved money is **not in `available_balance`**. Show "In payout: <payout_in_progress_kobo>" so the money does not look lost. Never add it to available.
- A `failed` or `cancelled` payout returns the money to `available_balance`; it is paid in the next batch (or an admin retries a failed one). Say "We could not pay out ... it is back in your balance".
- Show **Available** (cleared) and **Pending** (clearing) as two separate figures. Never add them together as "balance".
- For a pending earning show "Available on <next_available_at in the user's timezone>". `available_at` is a UTC time without an offset. A `held` earning has no date: show "On hold, support is reviewing it". A `voided` earning never becomes money.
- `total_earned` only counts released earnings; do not label it "lifetime earnings including pending".
- Saving a **different** bank account, or the **first** one while the wallet holds money, pauses payouts for 24 hours: **tell the caregiver before they confirm** ("You will not be paid for 24 hours after changing your bank account"; for a first account with money in the wallet, "Your balance will be paid out after 24 hours"). Show `payouts_blocked_until` when it is set.
- A caregiver never sees the admin-only `needs_attention` payout status. Their payout row stays `pending` (money still reserved) or shows `successful` (it was paid); do not tell them anything is wrong. Support handles it. A reversal that arrives after a payout was paid also leaves the row `pending` (the money is not returned until an admin has checked it). While an admin is checking, the caregiver's payouts are frozen: they are left out of the next batch and their available balance is not paid until the freeze is lifted. **The wallet response does not show the freeze** (no field was added; nothing here was captured live), so the app cannot explain it: say only that the payout is being checked and point to support.
- Always show the account holder's name from `resolve` and ask the caregiver to confirm before `PUT`.
- A caregiver cannot delete their account while any earning is pending/held, while there is a balance, or while a payout is `pending_approval`/`pending` (section 3, "Delete account"). A small balance under the minimum is stuck until it reaches the minimum or support adjusts it.

## 12. Field-nesting and unit traps

1. **Naira vs kobo.** `/caregiver/wallet` (including the new `payout_min_kobo` and `payout_in_progress_kobo`), `/wallet/transactions` and the action response's `*_kobo` fields are **kobo integers**. `/caregiver/home.wallet_balance_naira` and `GET /booking/booking/{id}` money fields (`total_amount`, `service_fee`, `platform_fee`) are **naira numbers**. The action response has naira as **strings** (`"55000.00"`).
2. **Same booking, three shapes:** the typed booking (`GET /booking/booking/{id}`, list endpoints), the raw action-response booking, and the notifications wrapper (nested `booking.patient_details` is `null`). See section 7.
3. **`visit_code`:** always present in typed bookings, `null` for everyone but the booking's patient (and only while `accepted`/`en_route`).
4. **Present-but-null:** `rejection_reason`, `changes_requested`, `location_updated_at`, `dispatch_state`, `patient_details`, `caregiver_details` are present with `null`, never absent.
5. **`caregiver_id` vs user id:** `/auth/me` returns both (`caregiver.id` and `user_id`). Booking `caregiver_id` is the **caregiver** id.
6. **Location `recorded_at`** does not move; use `location_updated_at`.
7. **Push `scheduled_at`** ends in `Z`; every other timestamp has no `Z`. All are UTC.
8. **`decline`** does not make the booking `declined`; it stays `pending`.
9. **`reason`** on decline and cancel is accepted and discarded.
10. **`channel`** is at the top level of the booking in the typed responses and inside `booking` in the action response; it was added later, so older captures of this guide do not show it. It is never null.
11. **Identity verification: the state object is flat on `GET`, `client-event` and `defer`, and nested under `state` on `POST .../sessions`.** `verification_id` and `widget` exist only on the session response. `active_verification_id` and `failure_hint` are present with `null`, never absent. `detail` is an object `{code, message}` on every identity-verification error but a string on the `403`, `401` and `410`.
12. **`defer` does not return the onboarding step.** Read `profile_completion_step` from `GET /caregiver/verification-status`.

## 13. State machines and polling

- **Offers.** Do not rely on push alone. While the app is open and the caregiver is available, poll `GET /caregiver/home` every **15 to 30 seconds** (one call returns offers, visits, availability and the wallet). Waves go out every 10 minutes, so offers appear in bursts; there is no faster signal.
- **Right after accept, `on_my_way`, `start`, `complete`:** use the `status` in the response, then refetch `GET /booking/booking/{id}`.
- **While a visit is active:** the booking detail is fine to poll every 15 to 30 seconds; the location goes over the WebSocket or REST ping.
- **After a `409` on accept** ("no longer available"): drop the offer and refetch home. Do not retry.
- **After a `423`:** stop sending codes until the `Retry-After` seconds pass; show the countdown.
- **Identity verification.** Read `GET /caregiver/identity-verification` when the identity screen opens. After the widget reports `completed`, poll it every **3 seconds, backing off to 10**, until `status` leaves `in_progress` or `active_verification_id` becomes `null`. After about 2 minutes say it is taking longer and let the caregiver leave; do not poll forever. A hint (`client-event`) never ends the wait. The backend decides when the provider's result is confirmed: poll until `status` is `verified` (go to consent), `needs_review` (say a person will check, then go to consent) or `last_attempt_status` is `failed` (show "Try again"). **After a decision the backend sets `profile_completion_step` to 3 itself for `verified` and `needs_review`** (not for "try again"): re-read `GET /caregiver/verification-status` rather than assuming the step. An attempt left unanswered for 24 hours becomes `expired` and does not count: show "That session timed out. Start again.". A push (`data.type` `kyc_verified`, `kyc_try_again`, `kyc_under_review`) is only a nudge to re-read the state.
- **Offers expire silently.** A booking nobody takes for 30 minutes becomes `unmatched`, but outstanding offers stay acceptable; a cancelled or taken booking returns `409`.

## 14. UX consequences the app must surface

1. **Going available needs a location, and a stale location means no offers.** If `is_available` is `true` but `location_updated_at` is older than about 15 minutes, show "You are not receiving offers: your location is out of date".
2. **Location pings must not carry the switch.** Omit `is_available` on every ping (foreground, background, WebSocket): omitted means *leave it as it is*. Only the availability toggle (`POST /caregiver/availability`) should change it (section 5).
3. **Only approved caregivers can act.** For `under_review`, `changes_requested`, `rejected`, `suspended`, disable the switch and accept; show the status screen. Offers are empty for them by design.
4. **"This job was taken by someone else"** on `409 Booking is no longer available`. **"You're not approved yet"** on the approval `403`s.
5. **Visit code lockout is real.** After 3 wrong codes the caregiver cannot start for 15 minutes, and the correct code is refused too. Tell them how many tries are left, then show the lock countdown, and tell them to ask the patient to check the code in their app rather than guess.
6. **The caregiver never sees the code.** Do not build a "show code" screen for the caregiver; the patient reads it out.
7. **An unaccepted offer carries no patient or caregiver details** (offer view, section 7). Build offer screens from `open_offers` or the offer-view fields only.
8. **Payouts are scheduled, approved by an admin, then sent.** There is no withdraw button. `pending_approval` is "being prepared", `pending` is "on its way"; only `successful` is "paid" (section 10).
9. **A push is a hint, not a guarantee.** Delivery was not verified, and the backend never retries or checks receipts.
10. **Cancelling depends on `channel`.** An `app` or `admin` booking can be cancelled from `accepted`, `en_route` and `in_progress`, and no reason is stored: confirm with the caregiver before sending it. A `whatsapp` booking can be dropped only while `accepted` and more than the policy lead (120 minutes by default) before the start; later, en route or in progress it is `409` "It's too close to the visit to cancel in the app. Please contact support." (section 7, "Cancelling").
11. **Deleting the account is permanent.** Warn before sending `DELETE /auth/profile`: the profile, bank details and verification data are erased and cannot be restored, and the same login cannot be used again unless they register from scratch. On `409`, show the `detail` text and send them to the booking or wallet screen it names. On `200`, sign out and clear everything stored on the device.
12. **Identity verification is decided by our partner, not the app.** Do not show "Verified" because the widget said success; wait for `status: "verified"`. Show no reason for a review (`needs_review`), and never speculate about one. If verification is down, offer "Continue without for now": the caregiver can reach consent but cannot be approved until verified (section 4).
13. **Changing the password signs out every other device**, and replaces this device's tokens too. Store the returned pair before anything else and say so in the success message (section 17).

## 16. Care plans (backend slices B1, B2 and B3)

A nurse records a care plan for a client; every caregiver on a live visit for that client can read it. Slice B1 (sections 16.1 to 16.12) is the plan itself: create, read, edit the header, allergies and special instructions, complete or archive, and the goals. Slice B2 (sections 16.13 to 16.20) adds the daily work: task edits, the day view, ticking, visit notes and progress. Slice B3 (sections 16.21 to 16.25) adds medications, a record only. **Not built yet** (later slices, do not code against them): the admin API, the family view. Ignore unknown fields in responses.

**Captured by the backend test harness (the real app and a real Postgres), not the compose stack**: captures `200` to `222` come from `app/tests/test_capture_care_plans.py` `230` to `238` from `app/tests/test_capture_care_plans_b2.py` and `240` to `244` from `app/tests/test_capture_care_plans_b3.py` (all skipped unless `CAPTURE_CARE_PLANS=1`). The data is placeholder; the nurse skill, bookings and clients were seeded in the database.

### 16.1 Who can do what

| Caller | Read a plan | Create or edit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caregiver **with the nurse skill** (assigned by an admin) | yes | yes | `can_author_care_plans` on Home is `true` |
| Caregiver without it | yes | `403` `not_a_nurse` | the free-text "specialization" never counts |
| A caregiver with no live visit for the client | `404` | `404` | see below |
| Patient token | `403` | `403` | `{"detail": "Access denied. Caregiver account required."}`; no token is `401` |

**Live visit** means a booking assigned to the caller whose status is `accepted`, `en_route` or `in_progress` (a suspended caregiver only `in_progress`) and whose patient account is active. This is the same rule as the client profile (section 7). **Access ends the moment the visit completes, is cancelled or the caregiver drops out**: the next call is a `404`, which is normal at clock-out, not an error to retry.

**One 404 for everything without access**: `{"detail": "Care plan not found"}`, whether the booking, plan, goal or client exists or not. A `404` on a plan call means "not available to you"; show "This care plan isn't available. You can see a client's plan while their visit is accepted, on the way or in progress."

Every successful care plan response carries `Cache-Control: no-store`. Health data: **do not write any care plan response to AsyncStorage or the query persister**; keep it in memory, with a short `gcTime`, and clear it on logout.

**Errors carry a code.** Care plan refusals (other than the plain `404` above and the `422` validation list) are `{"detail": {"code": "...", "message": "..."}}`. Match on `code`.

### 16.11 Traps and what the app must show

1. **`404` and "no plan" are different.** `GET /bookings/{id}/care-plan` answers `200` with `plan: null` when there is no plan; every other call answers `404` when there is no access. At clock-out every plan call becomes `404`: say so plainly, do not retry.
2. **`version` moves on every write**, including goal writes. After any write, take the new version from the response (`version` on the plan, `plan_version` on goals) before the next edit.
3. **Present-but-empty, not absent:** `allergies`, `special_instructions`, `goals`, `tasks` are `[]` when empty. `template`, `review_every_days`, `review_due_on` and `client.age` are `null` when unknown.
4. **`client`** is the person visited (the patient for a visit booked for themselves, the care recipient otherwise). There is no address, phone, email or booking condition in any care plan response.
5. **Do not show the nurse-only actions to others**: use `can_author` (visit summary) and `capabilities.can_edit_plan` (plan). Ticks and notes have their own flags (section 16.13). Show a read-only banner when `status` is `completed` or `archived`.
6. **Seeded templates are drafts**: show "Not yet clinically reviewed" while `clinical_review_status` is `pending`.
7. **Care plan responses are health data**: never persist them on the device.

### 16.19 B2 traps and what the app must show

1. **Never trust a tick locally.** Show a box as ticked only after the `200`. The response is the truth (`done`, `done_by`, `done_at`), including when another caregiver ticked it first.
2. **A tick can fail because the visit ended.** If the caregiver clocks out, every plan call becomes `404` and ticks `409` `visit_not_in_progress`. Prompt for notes and remaining ticks **before** clock-out.
3. **Only today.** Past days are history. Never show editable boxes on another day (`can_tick` is `false` there).
4. **Two caregivers, one box.** The second tick is a no-op that returns the first tick. Show "done by <name>"; do not show an error.
5. **Unticking is visible to the care team** (it is recorded). Ask for confirmation before unticking a task someone else did.
6. **A removed task leaves every list.** Its past ticks are kept by the server but the app cannot show them; do not offer to restore it (there is no such call).
7. **Task edits change every day.** Changing a task's days or slot applies to the whole plan, past days included. Warn the nurse before saving.
8. **Progress is not "days elapsed".** A low number early in a plan is normal; a day without a visit does not lower it. Explain "counted over visit days" next to the percentage.
9. **Notes are permanent.** Tell the user a note cannot be edited or deleted before they submit it.

### 16.21 B3 overview: medications (a record, nothing more)

Slice B3 adds three calls (add, edit, stop a medication) and a `medications` list on the full plan (`GET /caregiver/care-plans/{plan_id}`, the create and edit responses, and the `plan` inside a `409` `version_conflict`). Everything in 16.1 still applies: the same live-visit access, the same single `404`, `Cache-Control: no-store`, nothing written to the device.

**What this is, in the FE's terms.** A medication is *what the prescriber ordered, as the nurse wrote it down*. Oakly does not check doses, does not compare medicines, does not remind anyone and does not record that a dose was given (giving medicines is a visit task, ticked like any other task in section 16.16). There are no alerts to build and no "given" ticks.

**Show this notice on the medications section, always visible** (it is also in the OpenAPI description of the three routes and of the plan's `medications` field; the wording is pending clinical sign-off, so keep it in one string you can change):

> This is a record of what the prescriber ordered. Oakly does not check doses or interactions and does not remind anyone to give medicines. Follow the prescriber's instructions.

| Action | Who | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Read the list (on the plan) | any caregiver with a live visit for the client | a completed plan still shows its medications |
| Add, edit, stop | nurse | live visit (any status), plan `active` |

**The medication object** (the same in the plan's list and in every write response):

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | uuid | |
| `name` | string | 1-120 characters, the only required text |
| `strength`, `dose_amount`, `dose_unit`, `route` | string or `null` | up to 60 characters each. `dose_amount` is text so `"1/2"` fits. Not validated against anything |
| `reason`, `prescribed_by`, `note` | string or `null` | up to 200, 120 and 1000 characters |
| `frequency` | string | `once_daily`, `twice_daily`, `three_times_daily`, `four_times_daily`, `weekly`, `as_needed`, `other`. It does not have to agree with `times_of_day` |
| `times_of_day` | list of `HH:MM` | at most 12, no repeats, returned earliest first; `[]` for `as_needed` or `other` |
| `start_date` | date | defaults to today (Africa/Lagos) when not sent |
| `end_date` | date or `null` | never before `start_date`; `null` while ongoing |
| `status` | `active` or `stopped` | |
| `stopped_at` | datetime or `null` | UTC without a `Z`; set exactly when `status` is `stopped` |

**Order and history.** `medications` lists active medications first, then stopped ones, each group in the order recorded. A stopped medication is **never deleted**: the plan keeps what the client was on. Show stopped ones greyed under a "Stopped" heading. A plan holds at most 40 active medications (`422` `invalid_care_plan`); stopping one frees a place.

### 16.25 B3 traps and verification

**What the app must show or avoid.**

1. **Always show the notice** (16.21) on the medications section, including to caregivers who cannot edit. Do not paraphrase it into advice, and do not add dose warnings, "due now" states, reminders or "given" buttons: none of that is backed by anything.
2. **Do not treat a medication as an instruction.** It is a transcription. Do not compute "next dose" or "overdue" from `times_of_day`.
3. **Stopped is permanent.** Tell the nurse before confirming that a stopped medication cannot be edited or restarted; to restart, add a new one (the old one stays as history).
4. **Edits are last-write-safe only with the version.** Send the `plan_version` from the last response; on `409` `version_conflict` replace the screen with the `plan` in the body.
5. **Medications are health data**: never persist them on the device, and clear them on logout.
6. **Order matters to the screen, not to the data.** The list order is active-then-stopped; do not sort by `name` unless the design says so.

## 17. Change password: `POST /api/v1/auth/change-password`

Added with migration `0020_user_token_version`. **Source of these examples:** `scripts/capture_change_password.py` run against a real API process (uvicorn) on a real Postgres, not the compose stack used elsewhere in this guide; the files are in [`docs/fe-captures/caregiver/change-password/`](fe-captures/caregiver/change-password/). Tokens are replaced by `<TOKEN_n>` (the same `n` is the same token in every file) and passwords by `<CURRENT_PASSWORD>`, `<NEW_PASSWORD>`, `<THIRD_PASSWORD>`. The user's email, phone and ids in `me` are the throwaway ones the script registered.

### 17.1 What it is, and when the app calls it

A signed-in caregiver (the endpoint works for any app user) types their current password and a new one on a "Change password" screen. On success the backend **ends every session that existed before** (including this phone's own tokens) and answers with a **new token pair for this phone**, so the caregiver stays signed in here and is signed out everywhere else. Confirming the new password is the app's job: the backend takes two fields.

- **Auth:** `Authorization: Bearer <access token>`. Without it: `401` (below). A refresh token used as the bearer is refused as everywhere else (`401 Could not validate credentials`; tests only).
- **Request body:** `{"current_password": string, "new_password": string}`. Both required.
- **Password rules (server side):** at least 8 characters, at most 72 **bytes** (not characters: an accented character is 2 bytes, an emoji 4), at least 5 different characters. It must differ from the current one. There is no other rule (no digit or symbol requirement); the app may be stricter, never looser.

### 17.2 Success `200`

<!-- capture: change-password/08-success.json request.body -->
```json
{
  "current_password": "<CURRENT_PASSWORD>",
  "new_password": "<NEW_PASSWORD>"
}
```

<!-- capture: change-password/08-success.json response -->
```json
{
  "status_code": 200,
  "headers": {
    "content-type": "application/json",
    "cache-control": "no-store"
  },
  "body": {
    "access_token": "<TOKEN_5>",
    "refresh_token": "<TOKEN_6>",
    "token_type": "bearer",
    "detail": "Password changed."
  }
}
```

- The body is a **token pair plus a message**: `access_token`, `refresh_token`, `token_type`, `detail`. **Store both new tokens before doing anything else.** The `Cache-Control: no-store` header is there because the body carries credentials.
- **The tokens this phone used a moment ago are already dead** (capture 11 below). Any other request still in flight on the old access token will come back `401`, and refreshing with the old refresh token fails too (`401 Invalid refresh token`), which the app's current refresh handling treats as "session over, sign out". See 17.6 for what the app must do about that race.

### 17.5 Wrong current password is rate limited

Wrong current passwords are counted **per account** (not per device or IP). After **5** wrong ones in a row the account is in a wait: **every** call, even with the correct password, answers `429` with `Retry-After`. The wait starts at 30 seconds; each time it ends and another wrong password follows, it doubles (60, 120, ...) up to 15 minutes. Calls made during a wait are answered `429` and do not extend it. A success clears the count, as does an hour with no attempts. `weak_password` and `same_password` answers are **not** counted. The capture (a second caregiver) is five wrong passwords, each `400`, then the correct one:

### 17.6 UX consequences the app must surface (instructions, not trivia)

1. **Swap the tokens atomically and first.** On `200`, write the new `access_token` and `refresh_token` to secure storage, then release any queued requests. While the change request is in flight, hold other API calls (or at least do not let a `401` from one of them trigger sign-out): the old tokens die the moment the backend commits, so a request that was already on its way returns `401`, and its refresh attempt returns `401 Invalid refresh token`. Without this the caregiver is signed out by their own successful change.
2. **Say that other devices are signed out.** The success toast should read like "Password changed. You have been signed out on your other devices." It is true, and a caregiver who finds themselves signed out on a second phone will otherwise think something is wrong.
3. **The 429 needs a countdown, not a retry loop.** Disable the submit button for `Retry-After` seconds and say "Too many incorrect attempts". Do not retry automatically. A wait can be up to 15 minutes; the message is the same.
4. **Do not clear the form on `400`/`429`.** Keep what they typed (except show/hide state) and put the error on the right field (17.3).
5. **No "forgot current password" promise.** The existing forgot-password flow (section 3) does the job; link to it from the screen. Note that **resetting through the email link does not sign out other devices** (only this change does); that is a known follow-up.
6. **Never log or store the passwords** (including in crash reports and analytics), and do not keep them in state after the request ends.

### 17.7 Field-nesting and shape traps

- `detail` is an **object** on `400`/`429` from this route and a **list** on `422`; on `401`/`403` it is a plain string. A client that does `detail.toString()` shows `[object Object]`.
- The `200` body is a token pair **plus** `detail` (a string). It is the same shape as login's `Token` with one extra field, so the login response type cannot be reused as is.
- `Retry-After` is a **header** (whole seconds), not a body field. The `429` body has no wait field.
