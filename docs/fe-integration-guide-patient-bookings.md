## 1. What the feature is, in the app's terms

A family can cancel a booking from the app while it is **looking for a caregiver, accepted, or the caregiver is on the way**. Before they confirm, the app shows **what it would cost**. Cancelling costs nothing before a caregiver is assigned or more than 12 hours before the visit. Between 12 and 2 hours before, Oakly keeps **20% of the service fee**. Under 2 hours before, or once the caregiver is on the way, it keeps **50% of the service fee**. (The hours and percentages come from an admin-editable policy: read them from the response, never hard-code them.) The fee is taken from the **service fee only**, never from the platform fee.

**Cancelling never moves money by itself.** A paid booking gets a **refund request** (what was paid, minus the fee) that **a person at Oakly approves**, and it goes back to the **original payment method** (the card or bank used on Paystack). It is never automatic and never goes to an Oakly wallet. The app cannot speed it up; it shows the refund's status.

The same rules run for WhatsApp bookings (one backend function), so the app and WhatsApp never disagree.

Only the person who made the booking can cancel it. Once the visit has started it cannot be cancelled in the app: the family contacts support.

### 3.7 Field notes

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `can_cancel` / `reason` / `message` | `reason` is `null` when it can be cancelled, else one of `booking_in_progress`, `booking_completed`, `booking_already_cancelled`, `booking_closed`. `message` is safe to show. `booking_closed` (declined or otherwise closed) is **[not live]**: unit-tested only. |
| `fee_tier` | `none` (free), `standard` (the 12 h to 2 h tier), `late` (under 2 h, or the caregiver is on the way). |
| `fee_percent`, `policy` | The percent of the **service fee** kept, and the policy it came from (`free_hours`, `late_hours`, `fee_percent`, `late_fee_percent`). Build the wording from these. `policy` is `null` when `can_cancel` is false. |
| `fee_amount`, `fee_amount_kobo` | What Oakly keeps if the booking is cancelled now. |
| `paid_amount` | What was paid so far; `0` when the booking is not paid yet. |
| `refundable_amount` | What comes back: paid minus the fee. `0` when nothing was paid. |
| `refund_will_be_queued` | `true` for a paid booking. It is a **request for approval**, never an instant refund. |
| `refund_destination` | Always `"original_method"` (or `null` when no refund is queued). There is no wallet option; do not offer one. |
| `caregiver_assigned` | Whether a caregiver holds the booking (the caregiver is notified on cancel). |
| `as_of` | When the terms were worked out. They are a **preview**: send `accepted_fee_kobo` when confirming. |

## 4. Cancel: `POST /api/v1/booking/{booking_id}/cancel`

Request body (all optional; no body at all is fine):

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `reason` | string, max 500 | Why the family is cancelling. Shown to the person who approves the refund, and (new 2026-11-07) stored on the booking for every cancel, also a free cancel of an unpaid booking (section 5.2). Never returned by a booking view. |
| `accepted_fee_kobo` | integer, `>= 0` | The `fee_amount_kobo` the person saw. **Always send it.** If the fee is higher when the cancel is decided, nothing is cancelled and the answer is `409 terms_changed`. A lower fee is accepted. |

## 7. Field-nesting and shape traps

- **`dispatch_state` is not cleared on a cancelled booking.** A booking cancelled while it was still looking for a caregiver keeps `dispatch_state: "dispatching"` (capture 016). **Decide what to show from `status` first**; `dispatch_state` only matters while `status` is `pending`.
- **A cancelled unpaid booking keeps `payment_status: "pending"`** (capture 056) and `refund: null`. There is nothing to refund.
- **`detail` is an object on the cancel routes' errors, a list on `422`, a string on `401` and on the wrong-account `403`.** A client that does `detail.toString()` prints `[object Object]`; read `detail.code`.
- **`cancel_fee` `null` vs `0.0`:** `null` means the booker did not cancel it (or it is not cancelled); `0.0` is a free cancel.
- **`refund` is present-but-null** (not absent) when there is no refund.
- **Two money units on the cancel routes** (naira number, kobo integer) and **naira only on the booking**: never mix them; `accepted_fee_kobo` takes the kobo field.
- **The detail path has `booking` twice** (`/booking/booking/{id}`); the new routes do not (`/booking/{id}/cancel`).
- **`refund_will_be_queued` (terms) vs `refund_queued` (cancel result)**: same idea, different names, because one is a prediction and the other a fact.
- **`policy` and `message` are `null` when `can_cancel` is false.**

## 8. State machines and polling

Booking `status` as the app sees it: `pending` (paid and `dispatch_state: "dispatching"`, or unpaid) → `accepted` → `en_route` → `in_progress` → `completed`; `cancelled` and `declined` end it. **Cancel is offered in `pending`, `accepted`, `en_route`.**

Refund (on a cancelled paid booking): `under_review` → `processing` → `refunded`, with `needs_bank_details`, `declined`, `contact_support` as side exits.

Polling guidance (a suggestion, not measured):

- While a booking is `pending`, `accepted` or `en_route`, refresh the **detail** every 15 to 30 seconds (it carries the visit code and the status).
- After a cancel, the refund summary changes only when a person acts (minutes to days). Refresh it when the booking screen is focused, and at most once a minute; never poll the terms endpoint.
- The cancel screen should call `cancel-terms` when it opens and send `accepted_fee_kobo` on confirm. If it gets `terms_changed`, show the new fee from `detail.terms` and ask again.

## 9. UX consequences the app must surface

1. **Show the fee before the confirm button, in naira, with the tier's reason.** "Free", "20% of the service fee (₦10,000)" or "50% (₦25,000)". The refund line is `refundable_amount`. Build the explanation from `policy` (the hours and percentages can be edited by an admin).
2. **Never say "refunded" at cancel time.** A cancel queues a refund for a person to approve. Say "Your refund request has been sent for approval", and show `refund.status` afterwards.
3. **Say where the money goes:** back to the card or bank account they paid with. Do not offer a wallet or a "refund to wallet" choice.
4. **A changed fee is not an error.** On `409 terms_changed`, show the new fee from `detail.terms` and let the person confirm again.
5. **A double tap is fine.** Disable the button while the request is in flight anyway; if a retry comes back `already_cancelled: true`, show the same success screen (not an error).
6. **`booking_in_progress` is a handover to support**, not "try later": show `message` and a contact route.
7. **An unpaid booking that was "paid a moment ago":** read `refund_queued` on the response; if true, show the refund copy, not "cancelled, nothing to pay".
8. **`503 payment_check_unavailable`:** the booking was not cancelled. Offer "Try again".
9. **Only the booker may cancel.** Hide the cancel button for bookings the person did not make.
10. **Do not show `visit_code` anywhere but the detail screen, and only when the field is non-null.**

## 10. "Pay now" after an app restart: `POST /api/v1/booking/{booking_id}/payment-link`

`POST /booking/create` is the only call that used to return a payment link. If the family closes the app before paying, the booking is `status: pending`, `payment_status: pending` and the link is gone. Call this endpoint to get a link for it again. Only the booker may call it (same rule as cancel).

**The link is stored.** When `POST /booking/create` initialises the Paystack payment it keeps the `payment_url` and `payment_access_code` on the booking (migration `0023`), and this endpoint hands **that same link** back: Paystack refuses to initialise a reference it has already seen ("Duplicate Transaction Reference"), so the server never asks it to initialise the booking twice. The app does not have to keep the `/create` link in memory; it can ask for it whenever "Pay now" is tapped. The link is **only** in the `/create` response and this endpoint's response: booking detail and list always show `payment_url: null`.

**What is and is not proven.** Verified live against a local fake Paystack that, like the real one, refuses a repeated reference (examples below). **Not verified against the real Paystack:** that a stored checkout link still opens for the whole payment window, and what the real Paystack's checkout shows for a link that has been used. Bookings created **before** migration `0023` have no stored link; for them the server tries to initialise the reference, and against the real Paystack that is refused (`503 payment_link_unavailable`, 10.3). Their payment window is 30 minutes, so they disappear on their own after the deploy.

### 10.4 What the app must do

1. **Use the link from `/create` while the app has it; call this endpoint when it does not** (restart, list reopened). The server returns the same link, so both are equally good. Never take the link from the booking detail or list: it is `null` there.
2. **Show the time left** from `payment_expires_at` and stop offering "Pay now" when it passes. After `payment_window_expired` the only path is a new booking.
3. **After the person returns from Paystack**, call `POST /booking/booking/{id}/verify-payment` as before; this endpoint is not a payment check.
4. **`booking_already_paid` is good news.** Refresh and move on; never show it as a failure.
5. **Do not retry in a tight loop** on `payment_link_in_progress` or a 503: wait at least a second.
6. **Two links are one payment.** The same reference means Paystack charges it once, so the person cannot be charged twice by tapping again or reopening an old page.
